What changed, and why it looks like this

Five decisions logged on 2026-09-13, plus the Water ruling from the day before, worked into the frame from select-first.html. Every tile in the viewport is one authored tile from the 16 px sheet drawn below, chosen by the terrains at all four corners and placed whole. Paint with the six materials; switch to the face view and paint the cliff; paint cobble onto grass to see what an unauthored pair looks like.

Materials, not tiles
The Paint bar's first verb is Material. What you paint is a material per cell; the tile drawn at every cell corner is looked up from the four cells around that corner. A tile on the sheet is authored as the transition itself, half grass and half path, and carries a tag per corner saying which terrain is there. The renderer asks for the tile tagged exactly like the corner and places it whole.
Pairs are the cost
Every pair of terrains that can touch needs its own set of sixteen. The sheet below authors eight pairs. Paint a pair that is not authored and the demo falls back to each terrain's own edge set, so both sides get a rim, and marks the corner magenta; the status bar counts the missing pairs. That count is the artist's to-do list, and the setup screen is where it gets worked off.
Edge sets
Each terrain also has a set of sixteen where the other corners are nothing: the tile at the top of a cliff, or the edge of the volume. Those are the only tiles with transparent quadrants, and the quadrant that belongs to the lower cell is never drawn from them, because that cell draws its own corner from its own point of view.
Quarters
Each cell is drawn as four quarters, each the quadrant of the offset tile at its own corner. On flat ground that is pixel-identical to drawing the offset tiles whole, which is what the mesher does wherever the four cells are coplanar. At a cliff the split lands on the height change. The cliff-top cell sees the lower cell as nothing and draws its rim; the cell at the foot sees a taller neighbour of the same material as continuing and draws none, and a taller neighbour of another material as that material, so a sand floor meets a grass cliff with the sand-to-grass tile.
Priority is order
With authored pairs the list order no longer decides who draws over whom at a corner; the tile does. It still decides two things: which terrain is treated as the shape when a pair is authored from a template, and which terrain wins when three meet at one corner and the demo has to pick two.
Walls paint the same way
Face view shows one row's south-facing cliff as the grid it is: run along the face, whole-tile levels up. The Material brush on a band sets that band's voxel material; ⇧ clears it back to the column's voxels. Above the top band and below the bottom is nothing, so a wall material takes its lip and its base from its edge set, and stone meets cobble with the stone-to-cobble tile.
Built on voxels
The document becomes cubes, with a half slab, the 45° ramp and the half ramp as voxel shapes, and one material per voxel. Heights in the demo are whole cubes and Strength here rounds up to whole layers; the bar keeps half-tiles as logged.
Ramp by drag
With Ramp up, press on a cliff face and drag back onto the high side; the run grows one cell per cell of drag and the preview shows the 45° cut. A run that does not meet the drop is refused. Click any cell of a ramp to remove the whole run. Not in the demo.
Strength and height
Raise and Smooth carry a Strength. Flatten shows the Height it will set, sampled at the press and editable in place. Every one of these is a scrub field: drag sideways, click to type, arrow keys to nudge, ⇧ for coarse steps.
Water is a rail item
Fill and Drain, a Level scrub, the same stroke shapes as Terrain. Picking looks through water everywhere.
Stamp stays, for now
The raw tile brush is the third Paint verb, kept as an escape hatch. Its picker is the old sheet palette and is marked for redesign.

Terrain set setup, between RPG Maker and Tiled

Static mockup. Underneath is Tiled's model: every tile on a sheet carries a tag per corner, and the tile for a corner is the one tagged like it, so any tileset can be wired by tagging. On top is RPG Maker's: a template stamp that fills the tags of a 4×4 block from position, given the two terrains it transitions between, so art drawn to the template is wired in one click.
