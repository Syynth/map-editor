What changed, and why it looks like this

Four decisions logged on 2026-09-13, plus the Water ruling from the day before, worked into the frame from select-first.html. Paint in the demo to see the dual grid resolve; sculpt to see the rim rule at a cliff.

Materials, not tiles
The Paint bar's first verb is Material. What you paint is a library entry; the tile at every cell corner is derived from which of the four cells around that corner hold the material. Each material is sixteen whole tiles the artist draws, shown in the inspector as the 4×4 dual set. Corners come out of that with no corner art tagged by hand.
Quarters
The demo draws each cell as four quarters, each sampling the offset tile at its own corner. Toggle Show quarters to see the seams; on flat ground they are invisible, and at a cliff they land exactly on the height change, so nothing stretches. The cliff-top cell draws its rim; the cell at the foot treats a taller neighbour of the same material as connected and draws none.
Priority is order
Drag a material up or down the Materials list and it draws over or under its neighbours at a shared corner. There is no separate priority field. The count on each row is how many cells and bands use it, so an unused material is safe to delete.
Walls paint the same way
A cliff face is a grid too: run along the face, half-tile levels up. The Material brush on a band sets that band's wall material; the rest of the column keeps the cell's wall default, shown as the second swatch on each material row when it is used as a wall. ⇧ on a band clears the override back to the column default; ⌥ eyedrops from a top or a band.
Ramp by drag
With Ramp up, press on a cliff face and drag back onto the high side; the run grows one cell per cell of drag and the preview shows the 45° cut. Release when the run meets the drop; a run that does not meet it is refused and the status bar says by how much. Click any cell of a ramp to remove the whole run. No direction dropdown, no ramp panel.
Strength and height
Raise and Smooth carry a Strength in half-tiles per pass. Flatten shows the Height it will set, sampled at the press and editable in place. Every one of these is a scrub field: drag sideways to change, click to type, arrow keys to nudge, ⇧ for coarse steps. The size keys [ ] stay.
Water is a rail item
Fill and Drain, a Level scrub defaulting to the layer view's top handle, and the same stroke shapes as Terrain. Picking looks through water everywhere, so the ground under a pool is still what every other tool edits.
Built on voxels
Scheduled the same day: the document becomes cubes, with a half slab, the 45° ramp and the half ramp as voxel shapes, and one material per voxel. The sculpt verbs above are written against that, not against columns. Nothing in this frame changes; the demo stays top-down because the paint model is the same picture either way, offset tiles over a flat area and quarters where a height edge crosses a corner.
Stamp stays, for now
The raw tile brush is the third Paint verb, kept as an escape hatch. It stamps one tile from an authored sheet over a top or a band. Its picker is the old sheet palette and is marked for redesign; nothing else in the paint model depends on it.
